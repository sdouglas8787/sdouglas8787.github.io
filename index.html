About

I lead pricing strategy and commercial analytics for a global engineering business, and I build the tools underneath it. It is engineered-to-order: BOMs created daily, varying metallurgy, custom sizing, no two orders identical. I have worked the other end of it too, high volume and standard parts, a portfolio of well over a million lines.

Most pricing problems are not technology problems. They are governance, discipline and adoption problems; the technology just makes the fix scalable. The work below is what that looks like in practice. Every number states its basis, and where something cannot be claimed, it is not claimed.

How I work

Numbers carry their basis. A figure that cannot say where it came from does not go on the page. Where attribution is uncertain, the uncertainty is stated before anyone asks.

Tools check themselves before you have to. The gross-to-net bridge derives its own residual and reports it as its error rate, rather than absorbing it into a bar marked "other". The input cost tracker reproduces the published ONS headline from raw index levels before it asks you to trust anything else it says.

Stale data is a silent failure, so make it loud. Building the tracker, I found the ONS serves a frozen 2025 edition of the same series at a slightly different path: HTTP 200, plausible numbers, no warning of any kind. Every pipeline I ship prints the age of its data, because an endpoint that keeps answering with old numbers is worse than one that fails. The full write-up is in the tracker's README.

Validation is out of sample, or it is not validation. Walk-forward backtesting, never in-sample fit. I published my energy model's forecast errors and corrected for the measured bias, rather than refitting until it looked right.

Definitions before dashboards. Most analytics failures are definition failures. What a customer is, what a product is, which date counts: agreed and written down before anything gets built, because a better dashboard renders three incompatible numbers more confidently.

Track record

Manual pricing, from over 80% to under a quarter

I ran a programme in a large industrial business where most orders were priced by hand. Manual pricing fell from over 80% to under a quarter, and the share of products with no list price at all fell by more than half. The margin also improved over the same period, but several other things moved at once, so I would not claim the margin as a pricing result. The transferable part is the governance, not the number: a discount corridor nobody can quietly step outside, an audit with teeth, and a measure of whether the price you agreed is the price you got.

Price realisation as the global measure

Most pricing functions can tell you the price they agreed. Far fewer can tell you the price they actually achieved, because the two live in different systems and the gap belongs to nobody. I introduced price realisation as a global measure in a large industrial business. The interesting part was not the analysis, it was that it turned an opinion into a number people could argue about. A price increase that was agreed but never implemented is not a price increase, and until you measure that, nobody has to admit it.

An analytics platform where none existed

I built a pricing analytics platform in a large manufacturing business where none existed. Millions of transactions across two ERP systems, consolidated into curated datasets and pushed out through self-service reporting. The part that took longest was not the pipeline. It was getting agreement on what a customer was, what a product was, and which date counted. Most analytics failures I have seen are definition failures, not modelling failures, and this was no exception.

Country pricing factors that passed audit

A pricing model nobody can explain is a compliance risk, not just an analytical one. I inherited a set of country price multipliers that had not been touched in over a decade and had no documented derivation. I rebuilt them from public World Bank indicators - GDP at purchasing power parity, the Logistics Performance Index, tariff rates - as a transparent weighted model with guardrails you can see and change. It passed internal audit. The value was not a better number. It was that the new number could be explained, challenged and adjusted by someone who was not me.

Budgets within five percent, without buying the outcome

I built a cost-index-led budgeting methodology for a parts portfolio of well over a million lines. Revenue and profit came in within five percent of budget, and we did not use extra promotional activity to get there - which matters, because hitting a number by discounting into it is not accuracy, it is purchasing the outcome. The part I am most pleased with is that other markets adopted it. A method that only works when its author runs it is not a method, it is a habit.

Projects

Small, documented, reproducible tools that solve a specific commercial problem end to end. All public.

macro_chartbook

A designed one-page UK macro readout from public ONS and FRED series: rule-generated executive summary, YoY / rolling-12 / CAGR battery, and every chart carrying its source, series ID and vintage.

input_cost_tracker

UK input costs and pass-through from ONS producer price data. Prints a rule-generated reading of the month, validates itself against the published bulletin, and states which claims the data will and will not support. Standard library Python, no API key.

month_landing_forecast

Where does the month land, given today? A pre-month forecast from five years of history, blended with daily pacing so strong months are called up rather than missed. Walk-forward backtested; the months it cannot read are flagged, not hidden.

motorcycle_sales_forecast

UK motorcycle registrations forecast from scraped MCIA press statistics and Met Office data. The simple regression beats SARIMAX out of sample, and the weather variables are tested and mostly rejected rather than assumed.

uk_stock_tracker

Multi-retailer stock and price monitor. One consistent reading from eleven sources that each publish differently, with every reading labelled by the route it came from and how far it can be trusted.

g2n_waterfall

Gross-to-net and PVM bridges. Trade terms, conditional versus unconditional spend, attainment, year-on-year price growth. Built so it cannot quietly stop adding up, and so anything it cannot explain gets shown rather than buried.

home_energy_tariff_tracker

Forecasting engine for household energy costs. Walk-forward backtesting, bias correction, Monte Carlo tariff comparison and weather normalisation.

weather_dashboard

End-to-end pipeline: API ingestion, automated processing and Power BI reporting.
